## Division recurrence (`divide_recurrence`, t <= 2 terms)

- Seen: `divmod_t2_gap12` (x^13 + a x + b) 7.4% faster with `-falign-loops=32` under GCC 15.2
  (35.9 against 33.3 us at 2^14; every size), not under 16.2. The placement scan: one slow start of
  8, +8%. The hot code is the inner loop over the t terms (10 instructions, 2 turns a word) inside
  the loop over quotient words (26): slow where the inner loop starts at 28 mod 32 and spans 3
  blocks of 32 bytes, as 15.2 placed it. GCC does not pad an inner loop entered as often as it turns.
- Change, arm only: both terms every word, an absent one with e = k (never reached), so GCC
  unrolls the inner loop away; the loop on pointers (f read down, q written up) as a do-while after
  `simd::align_loop<kRecurrenceLoopNops>` (21 instructions). x86 runs the old loop.
- The new loop's starts (0-7 nops, `kscan-rec-16.2`, 3 interleaved runs): 4 mod 32 fastest; 8 and
  12 +0.1-0.9%, 0 and 16 +1-3%. kRecurrenceLoopNops = 2 starts it at 4 with GCC 15.2 and 16.2 (both
  put the same setup code in between; objdump). Against main: t2_gap12/14 27.8 against 33.3 us
  (16.2) and 35.9 (15.2), 2^18 447 against 530 and 573 us: 16-23% less time. `divmod_short`
  (x^3 + 2x + 5: each word waits for the one 2 below, a chain of reductions) unchanged, within 0.5%.

## Element-wise streams (lanewise: add, sub, neg, scale)

Not pinned. `add/14` was 11% faster with `-falign-loops=32` (16.2), and the placement scan spreads
`add`, `sub`, `neg` at 2^10-2^14 up to 1.37x. Scans of the library's loop with 0-15 nops before it
(`shift3.cpp`, both the current form and a do-while): add and sub slow where the 11-instruction form
spans 3 blocks of 32 bytes (+10-20%); the 9-instruction form fast at 0 mod 32 at 2^10 but at 32 mod
64 at 2^14, neg elsewhere again; the same 9 instructions in a driver of their own favour 24 mod 32.
No one start suits every op, size and binary, so a pin would only move the luck. scale (Shoup
product, multiply-bound): within 2% at every start.
