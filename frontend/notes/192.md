# #192: algebra: roots on backend/roots

2026-10-13, claude. Base `0d6a23e` (origin/main).

`fps::roots` (`include/fps/algebra.hpp`) is now one call into the backend's tangent Graeffe root
finder, `fps::root_finding::roots(f, out)` (`backend/roots/roots.hpp`, #122 r1, PR #188: lc-opt's
`polynomial_root_finding`). The frontend's root-only code is gone: x^z split off by hand, gcd(u,
x^(P-1) - 1) by `pow_mod`, Cantor and Zassenhaus by `split`, the sort. `factor` keeps `pow_mod`,
`Random`, `split` and `factor_square_free` until backend #123 lands.

## The wrapper

- f = 0 throws `std::domain_error` before the kernel (whose `std::invalid_argument` never
  escapes: out has the deg f words it asks for).
- deg f > `kMaxDegree` = 2^24 - 1 throws `std::length_error`, checked in the frontend before out
  is allocated (the kernel checks too, but after the caller has allocated out).
- f passed trimmed to deg f + 1 words; out of deg f words, resized to the count and
  `shrink_to_fit` (a random f has about one root; without it the result would keep 4 deg f bytes).
- Order (increasing, distinct) and the sizes are the kernel's: DESIGN.md's.

## The limit (DESIGN.md, Functions: a paragraph after gcd)

The kernel's transforms have 2 bit_ceil(d + 1) words, so it needs deg f <= 2^24 - 1, with x^z
counted (it checks deg f before splitting x^z off). The old path ran on u = f / x^z, any z, but
its squares modulo u have 2 deg u - 1 words, past the NTT's 2^25 for deg u > 2^24. So the inputs
that ran before and throw now: deg u = 2^24 exactly, or x^z u with deg u <= 2^24 < deg f.
Not kept: the old path is 6-114 times slower (below), so at those degrees it took seconds to
minutes (guess from the ratios: a random f of degree 2^24 tens of seconds, 2^24 distinct roots
far longer), and a second path for one degree is code to test for nobody. The kernel could split
x^z off before its check (a few lines in `backend/roots`, the backend coordinator's); not opened
as an issue: no caller needs it.

Checked by hand at the limit (Mac, M4 Pro, clang -O2, a one-off program, not committed):
x^(2^24 - 1) - 1 gives its 119 roots (gcd(2^24 - 1, P - 1) = 119) in 2.6 s; a random f of degree
2^24 - 1 two roots, both checked, in 1.6 s; peak RSS 2.8 GB (the kernel's per-thread workspace,
32 to 45 d words: in DESIGN.md).

## Tests (`frontend/test_algebra.cpp`)

Everything it tested (random f of sizes 0 to 129, four kinds, against gcd(f, x^P - x) by naive
powers; known roots with multiplicities times irreducible quadratics; x^4 - 1; 2000 roots), plus
`test_roots_long`: degrees 2^k - 1, 2^k, 2^k + 1 for k = 7, 9, 11 (the kernel's transform lengths
and wrapped coefficients), each planted (n factors over about 3n/4 values, 0 for odd n, times a
constant), times an irreducible quadratic, then with trailing zeros, and a random f of that
degree (every root found is a root; their count is deg gcd(f, x^P - x), x^P mod f by fps's
square and %); f = x^z u for z = 1, 2, 1000, 4097, x^z alone and x^z (x + 3); four threads
cycling over f of 100, 400, 1600 and 6400 roots (per-thread workspaces grown in turn). Errors:
deg f = 2^24 (unwritten memory, top word set) and x^(2^24) throw `std::length_error`; f of
2^24 + 5 words of degree 2 runs. `test_algebra` on the Mac: 0.55 s user at -O2 (0.13 s before).

`kernel.py test frontend` passes (-O2 and ASan/UBSan) on the Mac (scalar, arm), `lc-amd`
(scalar, sse, avx2), `lc-intel-test` (avx512), `lc-zen5-test` (zen5), `lc-arm` (arm).

## Against lc-opt

The API runs within 1% of the kernel from 2^8 on, so it keeps the kernel's ratios to lc-opt
(`backend/roots/notes/122-r1.md`, same run on Zen 3, `lc-bench`, GCC 15.2, fps sorted, lc-opt in
any order): random f 0.37-0.51 of lc-opt's time (warm: its RootFinder built once) at 2^6-2^12,
2^lg distinct roots 0.57-0.83, repeated roots 0.92-1.02; lc-opt's N = 4000 tests: all_distinct
1.014, all_same 0.745, max_random 1.000, max_repeated 0.994. Against lc-opt cold (its tables and
arena built per call, the judge's cost): 0.14-0.95. Our Zen 3 numbers here (107 ns per degree at
2^12 random, 210 split, 569 repeated) match the kernel's there (108 / 212 / 579 ns per degree).

lc-opt is wrong on split16 and split20: it returns 63383 of the 65532 distinct roots and 1014034
of the 1048030 (fps returns exactly the planted set). It was built for N <= 4000 and drops roots
in its climbs past that (guess: its tracked points or candidates overflow a bound; not chased,
lc-opt is not ours to fix). So for many roots past 2^12 there is no correct lc-opt time to meet;
fps's own cost there is #185. A random f runs at 0.34-0.39 of lc-opt's time at every size.
