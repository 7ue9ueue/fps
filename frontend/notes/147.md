# #147: division: divmod, f / g, f % g on backend/division

2026-10-12, claude. Base `99be240` (origin/main).

`include/fps/division.hpp` now runs every divisor it has no faster path for as one call into the
backend's kernel, `fps::division::divide` (#102, PR #144: lc-opt's blocked division on
`ntt::Transform`, a tuned plan per target), writing q and r into the returned memory. The
frontend's own Newton division (`series::inverse` and two products, 6-7 multiplies) is gone, and
so are `detail::division::{quotient, remainder, modulo}` (`modulo` had no caller since #141).

## Paths

One scan of g from the top finds deg g and its nonzero words below g[d], stopping at the 17th
(zero chunks of 64 words are skipped by one OR, which the compiler vectorizes). Then:

- g = c x^d: a copy (c = 1) or a scaling of f[d, n), and f mod x^d. Kept: the kernel takes 45-100
  times as long there (`divide_monomial@kernel`).
- t <= 16 terms below g[d], the highest at least 8 (t + 1) below it (`x^L - 1`, `x^d - x - 1` for
  d >= 25): long division in blocks of gap = d - (highest term) quotient words, which do not reach
  each other. Each block is scaled by 1 / g[d], then subtracted times each term from the rest
  below it (q and r hold the rest in place): t + 1 vector passes per word. New.
- Else t <= 2 terms (`x^3 + 2x + 5`): the recurrence Q[p] = c F[p] - sum (c g[j]) Q[p - (d - j)]
  on the reversals, then r[m] = f[m] - sum g[j] q[m - j]: the frontend's old few-term recurrence,
  now for the remainder too (no product), and with one reduction of a 64-bit sum per word.
- Everything else: the kernel, on g trimmed to its degree (the kernel needs g[m - 1] != 0, so its
  `std::invalid_argument` never reaches the user). `f / g` passes no remainder; `f % g` passes a
  quotient in `series::scratch` (k words).

How the bounds were chosen (same-run `<op>` against `<op>@kernel`, below):

- The old recurrence (up to 16 terms of G mod x^k) against the kernel: the kernel wins at 16 terms
  (g of 17 random words: kernel 2.1-7.4 ns per word of f, recurrence 9-13), at short quotients
  (g of n - 16: 0.5-2.3 against 8.7-13.2, the old path's remainder product was the slow part) and
  on Zen 3 and Zen 5 at 2 terms; the recurrence wins at 2 terms on Emerald Rapids (2.9 against
  3.6) and Neoverse V2 (4.0 against 7.4). So the recurrence stays for t <= 2 only.
- Blocks with gap 12 for 2 terms (4 (t + 1)) lost to the kernel on Zen 5 and Emerald Rapids (3.7-3.9
  ns against 2.1-3.7: blocks of 12 words, three calls each); at gap 24 (8 (t + 1)) they win on all
  four (0.29-0.79 of the kernel), and at 16 terms with gap 136 too (0.51-0.93). Close terms in a
  long divisor (16 terms within 68 of the top) go to the kernel.
- The recurrence first ran 2 times as slow as the old one under GCC (7-8.7 ns per word on every
  target, 2.1 under clang): `perf annotate` on `lc-intel` put the time on a branch GCC made of
  `x >= y ? x - y : x + P - y` (data dependent, mispredicted). Now each word is one `% P` of
  2 P^2 + c F[p] - (at most two products), below 3 P^2 < 2^62, with no compare.

Moving both few-term paths into the kernel's plan is backend issue #158.

## Errors and limits

g = 0 throws `std::domain_error` (the API's check, before the kernel). The old frontend threw
`std::length_error` for f of 2^25 + 2^20 by `x^(2^25) + x + 1` (its remainder product passed the
NTT's 2^25); the kernel splits its work into transforms of at most 2^25 words and has no such
limit there (a probe of its plan: f of 2^27 by g of 2^25 still has a plan; it throws only where no
plan fits), and that divisor now takes the blocks path anyway. The test now asks f of 2^25 + 2^10
by that divisor for q and r, checked at random points. DESIGN.md (Functions, the `divmod`
paragraph) now names the few-term paths and this.

## Tests

`frontend/test_division.cpp`: everything it tested, plus the paths' bounds (`test_sparse`: g =
c x^d plus t = 1, 2, 15, 16, 17 terms with the highest gap = 1, 15, 16, 23, 24, 127, 128, 135,
136 or d below d = 13, 30, 100, 140, 300, the others next to it or spread; f of d - 1, d, d + 1,
d + gap, d + gap + 1, d + 3 gap - 1, 5 d + 7), large sparse shapes (`x^(2^18) - 1` with f of
10^6 + 3, 16 random terms below x^300000), and the 2^25 case above. 16 mutations of the new code
all fail it (on the Mac, -O2): the blocks' bounds, the split between q and r, a dropped term or
scaling, the scan's stop and chunk, the monomial's c = 1 copy and zero fill, the recurrence's
offsets, bias, scaling, reversal and remainder taps, g not trimmed for the kernel. The bounds'
constants (gap factor 8, t <= 2) are speed choices: any value gives the same results.

`kernel.py test frontend` passes (-O2 and ASan/UBSan) on the Mac (scalar, arm), `lc-amd` (scalar,
sse, avx2), `lc-intel-test` (avx512), `lc-zen5-test` (zen5), `lc-arm` (arm). `test_division`
alone on `lc-amd` scalar under ASan/UBSan: 10.0 s user (main: 11.1 s), 3.4 s at -O2. Twice in
these runs, under load, an ASan/UBSan pass printed LeakSanitizer's "Unable to get registers from
thread" (once on `lc-amd` sse, once on `lc-zen5-test`) and still passed; 3 standalone runs of
each frontend sanitized test on `lc-zen5-test` did not show it again.

about 1% to the kernel (above), so `fps::divmod` keeps those ratios.
