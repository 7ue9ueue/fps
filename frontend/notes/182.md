# #182: composition: Taylor shift on backend/taylor_shift

2026-10-13, claude. Started on main `1e7e7a2`.

`include/fps/composition.hpp`'s Taylor shift (linear g, and the shift by g[0] before the
Kinoshita-Li kernel) is now one call into the backend's `fps::taylor_shift::shift` (#124: lc-opt's
polynomial_taylor_shift on every target). The frontend's own shift (factorial tables, scalar
weights, `middle_product`, fresh vectors per call) is gone, and with it the header's includes of
`fps/factorials.hpp` and `fps/products.hpp`.

## What the frontend does

- F = f(y + a) mod y^t, t = min(n, f.size()), a = g[0] != 0: the kernel on f without its trailing
  zeros (a scan from the top, O(trailing zeros)), into the first min(t, deg f + 1) words, zeros
  after. The kernel's `std::invalid_argument` (out longer than f) cannot happen.
- Linear g: the shift writes straight into the result (one allocation, unwritten memory, the tail
  past t zeroed), then h[k] *= b^k in a serial loop, skipped for b = 1 (`compose(f, x + c)`). The
  scaling stays in the frontend: the kernel has no scale (backend issue #186).
- General g with a != 0: the shift into an unwritten t-word buffer, then the composition kernel.
- Limit: the kernel takes f of at most 2^24 words (L = 2^lg >= 2 f.size(), at most 2^25). The
  frontend checks deg f < 2^24 itself and throws `std::length_error` ("fps::compose: Taylor
  shift ...") before any work, for any n. Before, the limit was f.size() + t - 1 <= 2^25 (the
  middle product), so a short n took f of up to 2^25 - n + 1 words; now 2^24 words past trailing
  zeros, whatever n. Trailing zeros no longer count (f padded past 2^24 words with zeros runs).
  a = 0 (scaling, or the kernel on f mod x^n) and constant g have no limit, as before. DESIGN.md
  (Functions, compose paragraph) says so, with the reason.

## Tests (`frontend/test_composition.cpp`)

- New `test_trailing_zeros`: f of 1, 7, 90, 200 nonzero-topped words plus 1, 60, 300 zeros,
  n in {1, 5, 150, 320}, g = a + b x, a + x, a + c x^2 and a random g with g[0] != 0, against
  Horner on `fps::mul` (the kernel's Horner path below 88 words and its lanes above; trimmed f
  shorter than t, as long, longer).
- `test_errors`: the shift's `std::length_error` from the frontend ("fps::compose: Taylor shift")
  for f = x^(2^24) at n = 2, 5, 2^23, 2^24 + 1 and on the general path; no limit with g[0] = 0
  (linear and general) or constant g; f = 1 + y^2 padded to 2^24 + 1 words through both paths.
  Replaces the old 2^25-word middle-product cases.
- `test_limits` (vector targets at -O2 only, as before): the shift at the kernel's kMaxSize,
  f = x^(2^24 - 1) padded with 5 zeros, g = 1 + x ((1 + x)^(2^24 - 1), binomials) and g = 1 + b x
  at n = 2^23 + 3 (times b^k), in a thread of its own (the kernel keeps 320 MiB per thread).
  Replaces the f = x^(2^24) case, which the kernel's limit no longer allows. Mac (M4, -O2):
  9.3 s for the whole test, 3.8 GB peak RSS.
- Mutation: without the zero fill past the kernel's output, 281 failures (ASan's 0xbe fill).
- `kernel.py test frontend` (-O2 and ASan/UBSan) passes on the Mac (scalar, arm), `lc-amd`
  (scalar, sse, avx2), `lc-intel-test` (avx512), `lc-zen5-test` (zen5), `lc-arm` (arm). One arm
  ASan run on `lc-arm` printed LeakSanitizer's "Unable to get registers from thread" and passed
  (seen before, #147, #169).

## Bench (`frontend/bench_composition.cpp`)

`compose_linear@kernel/<lg>` is now the kernel's shift on the same f and a into kept memory (it
was `ntt::middle_product` of the old shift's shapes). New: `taylor_shift/<lg>` (compose(f, a + x):
the shift alone, b = 1) with `taylor_shift@kernel/<lg>` (the same kernel call). n = 2^(lg-1).

`kernel.py bench frontend --filter '^(compose|taylor_shift)' --rounds 7 --base c826915` (main
`1e7e7a2` with this bench file, so the old frontend runs the same cases), GCC 15.2; `lc-bench`
avx2 (EPYC 7B13, Zen 3), `lc-intel` avx512 (Xeon 8581C), `lc-zen5` zen5 (EPYC 9B45), `lc-arm` arm
(Neoverse V2).

## Against lc-opt

From the kernel's same-run comparison (`backend/taylor_shift/notes/124-r1.md`, `lc-bench`, outputs
checked equal): fps / lc-opt 0.994 at n = 2^19, 1.005 at 400000, 1.006 at 2^17, 0.998 at 100000,
1.005 at 2^15; 0.31-0.93 below 2^14 (lc-opt's fixed costs); 0.52 at 2^16 and 2^18 (lc-opt runs
even lg only). `fps::compose(f, x + c)` runs at 1.00 of the kernel (above), so it keeps those
ratios. `compose(f, c + b x)` with b != 1 adds the scaling (#186); lc-opt has no such problem.
