# #168: algebra: gcd, inverse_mod on backend/gcd

2026-10-13, claude. Base `25e3095` (origin/main).

`fps::gcd` and `fps::inverse_mod` (`include/fps/algebra.hpp`) are now one call each into the
backend's half-gcd, `fps::half_gcd::gcd` and `fps::half_gcd::inverse_mod` (`backend/gcd/gcd.hpp`,
#103, PR #156: lc-opt's half-gcd on `ntt::Transform`'s values form). The plain Euclid on `f % g`
and the extended Euclid on `divmod` are gone. `roots` and `factor` call `gcd` unchanged.

## The wrapper

- gcd: f and g as spans trimmed to their degrees; the result is allocated unwritten, of as many
  words as the gcd can have (the shorter trimmed size if both are nonzero, else the nonzero one's,
  which is what the kernel asks for), and resized to the kernel's count. gcd(0, 0) has no
  coefficients, as before.
- inverse_mod: m = 0 throws `std::domain_error` (the API's check). m trimmed to d + 1 words (the
  kernel needs its top word nonzero), h of d words, the kernel's -1 gives `std::nullopt`, and a
  constant m gives d = 0 words (the kernel returns 0 for m of one word). f trimmed too: the kernel
  checks f's length against the transforms' 2^25 before trimming, so trailing zeros would throw.
- The kernel's `std::invalid_argument` (out short, m's top word 0, h of the wrong size, scratch
  short) cannot reach the user: the frontend passes exactly those sizes and no scratch.
- New: degree 2^25 or more throws `std::length_error` (the kernel's `check_length`), also for
  gcd(f, x + 1) or inverse_mod(f, x^2 + 1) with a long f, which the old code answered through one
  long division. Documented in the header and DESIGN.md (Functions, a paragraph after `sqrt`);
  backend issue #170 asks the kernel to check only the shorter input.

## Tests (`frontend/test_algebra.cpp`)

Everything it tested (sizes 0 to 129, both argument orders, kinds, trailing zeros, common factors,
Fibonacci-like chains, inverse_mod by f h = 1 mod m), plus `test_long`, past the kernel's Euclid
base (64 degrees): f of 2^k - 1, 2^k, 2^k + 1 words for k = 7, 9, 11 by g of n/2 to n words,
inverse_mod checked by f h = 1 mod m and gcd = 1, then both times a random common factor c (gcd =
monic c against naive Euclid, both orders, inverse_mod nullopt); gcd(x^a - 1, x^b - 1) =
x^gcd(a,b) - 1 for (4096, 1536), (5000, 3001), (65, 64), (3000, 2999), and no inverse; a common
factor of degree 1500 in polynomials of 4097 words; f of 2^25 + 3 words of degree 1 (trailing
zeros: no throw). Errors: degree 2^25 for f or g of gcd and f or m of inverse_mod throws
`std::length_error` (unwritten memory, the top word set). A mutation (passing f untrimmed to the
kernel) fails it. `test_algebra` on the Mac: 0.13 s user at -O2.

`kernel.py test frontend` passes (-O2 and ASan/UBSan) on the Mac (scalar, arm), `lc-amd`
(scalar, sse, avx2), `lc-intel-test` (avx512), `lc-zen5-test` (zen5), `lc-arm` (arm).

`roots` and `factor` gain 3-35%: their time is in the powers modulo f (`pow_mod`: about 30

0.408 (lc-arm).

## Against lc-opt

The API runs within 1% of the kernel from 2^8 on, so it keeps the kernel's ratios to lc-opt
(`backend/gcd/notes/103-r1.md`, same run on Zen 3, `lc-bench`, GCC 15.2): inverse_mod at 1.029 of
lc-opt's time at 2^6, 1.014 at 2^8, 0.985 at 2^10, 0.967 at 2^12, 0.955 at 2^14, 0.942 at 2^16,
0.932-0.937 at 2^18-2^20, 0.944 at lc-opt's 50000. lc-opt has no gcd; fps's gcd takes 0.88-0.89
of lc-opt's inverse_mod time. Our Zen 3 numbers here (204 ns per degree at 2^14 for inverse_mod)
match the kernel's there (203.9).
