n = L + r, L = bit_floor(n). Every op ran Newton steps on powers of two to L and a last step at
length L whose cost did not depend on r:

- inverse: the half step (`inverse_half_step`, 6 transforms and 3 leaf products of L) for r <= L/2,
  0.75 of the time of the inverse to L on the Mac (5.58 of 7.45 ms at 2^19); a step at 2L above.

- log and pow: the division's last block, of c = count - (B - 1) k words, ran its residual and its
  product by h at 2k whatever c; at count = 2^j + 1 that was a third block at twice the length of

## What went in

1. inverse: the ragged chain (`inverse_chain`). Step lengths halve down from n (ceil(n / 2^j)), so
   they land on n; a step from k = ceil(n'/2) to n' = L' + r', r' <= L'/2, runs at length L'
   (`inverse_ragged_step`): with f_lo = f mod x^L', the cyclic product of f_lo and g mod (x^L' - 1)
   is exact on [k, L') and holds [i = 0] + (f_lo g)[L' + i] on [0, r') (f g = 1 mod x^k), and (f
   g)[L' + i] adds (f_hi g_r)[i]; then g e = g e1 + x^(L' - k) g e2 with g e1 of L' - 1 words (exact
   at L') and (g_r e2) mod x^r'. The power-of-two step's 5 transforms and 2 leaf products of L', on
   n' words instead of L', plus two products of r' words (the corners). Steps with r' > L'/2 (small
   lengths only) run at bit_ceil(n') (`inverse_plain_step`). Used for r <= 9L/32; above, the old
   steps (the corners cost more than they save there: sweep below).
2. Short products (`low_product`): (a b) mod x^c by sums to 32 words, `small::multiply` where it
   beats the NTT, else the NTT's ragged product (#216) through `ntt::detail::ragged_into` (a split
   plan) or the variant's product cut to c (a plain plan) in work the caller gives: `ntt::multiply`
   takes this thread's region, which series holds during an op, so it would map a fresh one (page
   faults) per call. Where the plan for the corners' 2r - 1 words is plain, inverse runs both as
   cyclic products sharing one transform of g mod x^r (a transform fewer).
3. Windows (`window()`): (a b)[pos + i] for a few i by sums of Montgomery products, b read
   backwards by a lane reversal, 4 positions a pass. To 16 coefficients (`kDirectWindow`) a last
   step's window of a full-length product is that, not the product.
4. exp's last step: block 2 of c2 coefficients takes r mod x^c2 from a window (c2 <= 16, no
   transform in the block) or a cyclic product with c2 words out, and t = h0 r, g_2 = g0 s mod x^c2
   as low products to c2 <= 9m/32 (instead of cyclic products of m with H and Gt); block 3 the
   same (its S window to 16, its two products as low products). sqrt's last step the same: (g^2)[m,
   m + rest) by a window, d = h r and d1 as low products. In both, when the last step is light (c
   <= m/4, no H or Gt read), the step before runs as a last step too (`exp_light`, `sqrt_light`):
   exp's last-step form to 2m is 14 transforms against a full step's 16, sqrt's 8 against 11, and h
   mod x^(m/2) is all the light step reads.
5. The division (log, pow): a last block of c words runs q_c = h_c (residual) mod x^c as a low
   product (h mod x^c copied before T(h)) where `short_product` holds at 2k, and to 16 words its
   residual by a window of q's coefficients (log: from g) with f, so it needs no W_(B-1), no
   T(q_(B-2)) and no Toeplitz product. And 5 blocks of k/2 where 3 blocks of k would leave a short
   one (count in (4k', 5k'], k' = k/2; e.g. 2^j + 1, 1.25 2^j, 6e5): 28 transforms and 17 leaf
   products of length k against 18 and 8 of length 2k (notes.md's counts). pow passes exp's last
   step its kept transforms only when they have its length (not for 5 blocks).
