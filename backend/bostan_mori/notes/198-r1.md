## 1. Why 16.2 is slower on short fractions

Two causes, one per path.

**`coefficient/2` (Fibonacci, 60 steps of `detail::tiny`)**: GCC 16's induction-variable
optimization (ivopts) rewrites the exit tests of tiny's inner sums (`for (i = lo; i < nq && i <= t;
++i)`, bounds that change with each word and with k's bit r). 15.2: the loop rotated, `i < nq` as
the back edge (`cmp; jb`), `t < i` as the exit (`jb`), the first test hoisted (`setae`/`test`).
16.2: `i < nq` became `i == nq` (`je`, tested first), then `t >= i` as the back edge (`jae`), the
loop entered by a `jmp` to its second test, the start index by `cmovae` (`asm/tiny-avx2-15.s`,
`asm/tiny-avx2-16.s`). Same instruction count; on Zen 3 it runs 1.5-2.8x slower:

| old tiny, Zen 3 | 15.2 | 16.2 | 16.2 -fno-ivopts | 16.2 -falign-loops=64 | 16.2 -falign-jumps=32 | 16.2 -fno-reorder-blocks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Fibonacci, k = 10^18 | 1142 | 1708 | 1129 | 1487 | 1801 | 1694 |
| Fibonacci, k = 2^60 - 1 (r = 1 always) | 1052 | 1775 | 1011 | 1610 | 1958 | 1824 |
| Fibonacci, k = 2^59 (r = 0 always) | 1058 | 1726 | 1009 | 1444 | 1593 | 1772 |
| p 1 word, q 2 words, k = 10^18 | 749 | 1779 | 747 | 1743 | 1756 | 1755 |
| p 1 word, q 2 words, k = 2^60 - 1 | 732 | 739 | 680 | 722 | 739 | 742 |
| p 1 word, q 2 words, k = 2^59 | 729 | 2019 | 670 | 1983 | 1989 | 2016 |

`-fno-ivopts` alone gives back 15.2's times (0.92-0.99 of them); alignment and block order do not.
Constant bits of k (every step the same r, so the same trip counts) regress as much as 10^18: not
a misprediction of k's bits. Which exit each loop takes matters: with p of 1 word, all-odd steps
are as fast as 15.2 and all-even ones 2.8x slower. The Zen 3 mechanism is not measured: GCP's AMD
VMs have no PMU (`lc-bench` lists no `cpu` event source). The other machines barely care
(`tiny.cpp`, Fibonacci at 10^18, 16.2 / 15.2): Emerald Rapids 0.93, Zen 5 1.09, Neoverse V2 0.97.

**`coefficient/16` (schoolbook steps)**: 16.2 turns the two per-step loops of zero stores
(`for (j = nu; j < 2 extent; j += W) store(out + j, zero)`, 1-3 vectors) into calls of `memset`,
with `vzeroupper` and four ymm (four q on Neon) spilled and reloaded and two constants rebuilt
around each call; and the five clears of the stack arrays at the start (`std::fill_n`, 1216 bytes
each) into `memset` calls instead of `rep stos` (x86). `call` in `Schoolbook::run` 5 -> 12 (x86),
`bl` 10 -> 12 (arm; 15.2 already called memset for the clears there). A fixed cost per step, so
short fractions lose most (`school.cpp`, 16.2 / 15.2):

| d | 4 | 6 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 24 | 32 | 64 | 96 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Zen 3 | 1.01 | 1.01 | 1.05 | 1.04 | 1.01 | 1.02 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| Emerald Rapids | 1.10 | 1.12 | 1.38 | 1.13 | 1.07 | 1.07 | 1.02 | 0.97 | 0.98 |
| Zen 5 | 1.04 | 1.04 | 1.01 | 1.04 | 1.04 | 1.04 | 1.02 | 1.01 | 1.00 |
| Neoverse V2 | 1.13 | 1.11 | 1.12 | 1.08 | 1.06 | 1.03 | 1.02 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

`-fno-ivopts` does not help here (Zen 5 d = 96: 1.37 with it). Large d (values path) does not move.

## 2. Kept

1. **Tiny steps on fixed shapes** (`tiny_steps<NP, NQ>`, `kTinyMax` 4 -> 8). p of NP and q of NQ
   words with NP = NQ - 1 (p shorter than q, zero-padded to NQ - 1) or NP = NQ (q zero-padded):
   a step keeps the shape for either r, so the whole call is one template with every loop
   unrolled at compile time (`unroll<N>`, a fold over an index sequence), p, q in registers. r
   picks p_(b + r) by a mask (`lo ^ ((lo ^ hi) & -r)`): no branch on k's bits and no loop of
   variable length for a compiler to rewrite. V[j] = s_j q_j + 2 sum over i < j of s_i q_(2j - i)
   (q(x) q(-x) is even: half the products). No cut by k inside (only the call's trim): the steps
   are exact either way. 15.2 and 16.2 build it within 3.5% of each other at d <= 5 except
   Neoverse V2 at d = 2 (16.2 11% faster), and 16.2 is 2-25% faster at d = 6, 7 (`tiny.cpp`,
   `tiny2.cpp`). Against main (old tiny for d <= 3, schoolbook for 4-7), p of d and q of d + 1
   words, q[0] = 1, k = 10^18, new 16.2 / main 15.2 / main 16.2:

   | d | Zen 3 | Emerald Rapids | Zen 5 | Neoverse V2 |
   |---:|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | 285 / 739 / 1717 | 229 / 547 / 545 | 207 / 530 / 532 | 324 / 650 / 651 |
   | 2 (Fibonacci) | 388 / 1146 / 1750 | 297 / 992 / 936 | 240 / 788 / 921 | 391 / 964 / 932 |
   | 3 | 565 / 1512 / 1574 | 435 / 1282 / 1232 | 293 / 1087 / 1121 | 511 / 1241 / 1319 |
   | 4 | 813 / 1788 / 1809 | 605 / 1599 / 1789 | 393 / 1429 / 1481 | 665 / 1692 / 1907 |
   | 5 | 1072 / 1853 / 1869 | 790 / 1631 / 1814 | 558 / 1450 / 1498 | 811 / 1773 / 1919 |
   | 6 | 1280 / 1915 / 1917 | 1021 / 1640 / 1844 | 657 / 1483 / 1535 | 1022 / 2052 / 2288 |
   | 7 | 1621 / 1940 / 1926 | 1239 / 1729 / 1938 | 868 / 1406 / 1584 | 1271 / 2088 / 2145 |

   Fibonacci: 2.95x (Zen 3), 3.3x, 3.3x, 2.5x faster than main with 15.2. A step at d = 2 is
   6.5 ns on Zen 3: 18 multiplies (8 products, 5 reductions by `% P` of 2 each), branch-free.
   Kept up to 8 words: at d = 7 still 1.2-1.6x faster than schoolbook steps on every machine
   (Zen 3 the closest, 1621 against 1926).
2. **No inverse when q[0] = 1** (every linear recurrence). tiny: q[0] after h steps is
   q0[0]^(2^h), exact, so 1 stays 1 and the answer is p[0]. Schoolbook: q[0] after h steps is
   q0[0]^(2^h) 2^(-32 (2^h - 1)) (squared and times 2^-32 per step), so for q0[0] = 1 its inverse
   is a table entry (`kUnscale[h]`). The inverse (30 dependent squarings) costs 112 ns on Zen 3,
   98 on Emerald Rapids, 87 on Zen 5, 130 on Neoverse V2 (`tiny.cpp`, `inverse`); with q[0] = 5
   the new tiny pays it (`tiny2.cpp`, `/q0` cases: +100, +80, +78, +130 ns).
3. **Schoolbook: loops, not memset calls.** `asm("" : "+r"(j))` in the zero-store loops (the
   idiom `backend/small` uses for its copies), and the clear at the start only where windows reach:
   [-W - 1, halves + W + 1) of each half (`kFront + written + 2W + 16` words, was all 304 of each
   of five arrays). Reads past that are caught by `test_steps.cpp` (the clear without the 2W + 16
   fails it).

## 3. Tried, not kept

- **Masked last block** instead of the zero stores past nu and nv in `out` (`first(a, n)` by
  sub/shr/min, so the split's loads read one aligned store each: the zero store past nu, at any
  offset, overlapped them, and Emerald Rapids spent 10% at d = 16 on the loads after it, `perf
  annotate`). `school2.cpp`, masked / unmasked, 16.2: every block masked Zen 3 0.96 (d = 8) to
  1.11 (d = 96), Emerald Rapids 0.93 to 1.05, Zen 5 0.83 to 1.03, Neoverse V2 1.01 to 1.05; only
  the last block masked Zen 3 0.96 to 1.10, Emerald Rapids 0.92 to 1.05, Zen 5 0.81 to 1.00,
  Neoverse V2 1.01 to 1.08. Built with 15.2, the masked last block was 0.93 to 1.02 on Zen 3
  (unmasked, 15.2 and 16.2 tie within 1%): the loss at large d with 16.2 is the code of the term
  loop around it more than the masks. With the masks, the stall moved to the first window load of
  the next step (7.6% of the cycles at d = 16): windows at any word offset read halves just
  written by vector stores. Neoverse V2 loses at every d: no gain to trade there. Files:
  `bostan_mori-mask-variants.hpp`, `mask*.txt`.

## Next

- Schoolbook steps at d = 9-95: per step at d = 16 on Emerald Rapids 205 cycles, 1083
  instructions (IPC 5.3, `perf stat`), 204 `vpmuludq`: blocks of W words take every term whose
  window meets them, about 3x the 70 a half-size product needs, and V's symmetry (q(x) q(-x) is
  even) is unused. The first window load of each step waits on the stores of the split before it.
  Windows from registers (`valignd`, `ext`) for halves of at most 2-4 vectors would avoid both.
- A faster inverse (30 dependent squarings, 87-130 ns) would help q[0] != 1 and the values path's
  last step; Montgomery squarings in [0, 2P) save about 1 cycle of 12 each.
