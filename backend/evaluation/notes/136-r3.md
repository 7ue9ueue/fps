## Kept

1. **Leaf products by target** (product_tree's `leaf_products_k`, product_tree/notes/136-r3.md):
   rows of 16 words, one 64-byte line each (were 24: half the rows' loads crossed a line);
   Neon two leaves per step with b_i an element of b's vectors (`umlal` by lane, no broadcasts,
   8 sums); Zen 4/5 two leaves per zmm (`kWideLeaves`, ntt's WideBottom windows). ns per word,
   kernel alone at 2^12 / 2^17 / 2^20 (`leaf3.cpp`), old -> new:
   - Zen 3: 0.818 / 0.826 / 0.826 -> 0.797 / 0.804 / 0.805 (rows).
   - Emerald Rapids: 0.691 / 0.692 / 0.717 -> 0.657 / 0.659 / 0.685 (rows; zmm pairs 0.78-0.82).
   - Zen 5: 0.516 / 0.521 / 0.515 -> 0.411 / 0.428 / 0.416 (zmm; rows alone 0.99).
   - Neoverse V2: 1.473 / 1.474 / 1.480 -> 1.242 / 1.246 / 1.253 (by lane; 4 leaves a step 1.33,
     rows alone 1.00).
2. **Two products of one window** (`leaf_products_twice`): the descent's root and halves multiply
   one state by both siblings' kept transforms (4 of the descent's 6 leaf-product passes of m8
   words); one window per leaf serves both, so the window's loads and its w A product are shared.
   ns per product at 2^12 / 2^17 / 2^20: Zen 3 0.731 / 0.741 / 0.744, Emerald Rapids 0.573 /
   0.585 / 0.589, Zen 5 (zmm) 0.324 / 0.336 / 0.332, Neoverse V2 (by lane, one leaf per step)
   1.150 / 1.152 / 1.155. With item 1, A/B against d708598 (5 rounds), evaluate 12 / 17 / 20:
   Zen 3 1.000 / 1.000 / 1.002, Zen 5 0.951 / 0.976 / 0.986, Neoverse V2 0.974 / 0.985 / 0.991
   (Emerald Rapids within its noise, 0.98-1.04); interpolate and newton alike, less (fewer of
   their passes are twins).
3. **Interpolation in groups of bases** (`interpolate_group`, `kInterpolateGroup` = 64 slots): a
   lane node of at most 64 slots runs its descent to all its bases' values M'(a_i) first, each
   base's products kept (`Kept`, in the stack), then one `divide_slots` for all of them (one power,
   8 chains), then its sums (`group_sums`). Each base had its own inversion, whose power (about 30
   dependent Montgomery steps) the out-of-order core could not overlap: the next base's work came
   after it. Steps, interpolate at 2^12 / 2^17 / 2^20, 5-7 rounds:
   - two sibling bases sharing one power, against none (with item 5): Zen 3 0.983 / 0.992 /
     0.989, Zen 5 0.959 / 0.970 / 0.982, Neoverse V2 0.993 / 0.999 / 0.996, Emerald Rapids 1.01 /
     0.994 / 0.996 (noisy);
   - groups of 128 slots against the pair: Zen 3 1.013 / 1.002 / 1.003, Zen 5 0.968 / 0.982 /
     0.980, Neoverse V2 1.000 / 0.998 / 0.999;
   - 64 against 128: Zen 5 0.944 / 0.973 / 0.983, the others 0.99-1.01; 256 against 128 0.98-1.01;
   - 64 against 32 (the same code, two bases a group): Zen 3 0.994 / 1.002 / 0.998, Emerald
     Rapids 0.990 / 1.002 / 1.000, Zen 5 0.978 / 0.991 / 0.990, Neoverse V2 0.996 / 1.001 / 0.996.
   So 64 everywhere. Zen 5 gains most, its other work being the fastest. Not understood: why 64
   beat 128 there by 2-6% (a guess: a group of 128 keeps 8 bases' products, 31 KB, past its L1
   with the rest).
4. **The Newton basis' combine deferred** (x86, `kDeferredCombine`): a lane node's halving combine
   goes into the child's one product (`combine_product`: x = s_lo -+ s_hi written back, and x Q_r),
   as the descent's since round 2; also the lane root's alternating signs (C = L). Newton at 2^12 /
   2^17 / 2^20, 7 rounds against item 2: Zen 3 0.997 / 0.996 / 0.995, Emerald Rapids 0.996 / 1.001
   / 1.004 (noise), Zen 5 1.003 / 1.000 / 1.000.
5. **The root's window read in place** (`forward_window`): when C = L the window fills the
   transform, so `Transform::forward(span, F)` reads it from the division's output (no copy; the
   Newton basis' rightmost known window of factor 1 too, no scale pass). With item 3's pair, A/B
   against item 4 (7 rounds), evaluate 12 / 17 / 20: Zen 5 0.986 / 0.990 / 0.991, Zen 3 0.999 /
   1.003 / 0.999, Neoverse V2 0.999 / 1.002 / 1.000.

  for one leaf per ymm with 64-byte rows (as ntt found for its WideBottom, #98 r1).
- **kNewtonPad refit** (round 2's lead: C < L costs a lanes inverse at the leaves): newton_tree
  with C slots against bit_ceil(C), n = r 2^lg for lg 12-18 and r 0.52-0.95 (`pad.cpp`), the
  ratio plain / padded crosses 1 at r = 0.70-0.75 (Zen 3, Zen 5, small sizes) to 0.65 (2^18); at
  0.60-0.65 on Emerald Rapids and Neoverse V2. With 0.65 the worse choice costs at most 2% (Zen 3
  and Zen 5 at r = 0.70 for n <= 2^15), and a higher value would cost as much at 2^17-2^18: 0.65
  stays.
- Not tried, by arithmetic:
  - **The top tree in ntt's values form** (round 2's lead): with the twin products the descent's
    leaf products cost 0.33 (Zen 5) to 1.15 ns per word per product, so the values form saves less
    than round 2 estimated: Zen 5 about 1% of evaluate and 2% of interpolate, Zen 3 nothing, and
    the leaf form stays for m8 >= 2^24. Left.
  - **A fused R_l Q_r + R_r Q_l leaf pass** for the interpolation's top (one kernel for its two
    leaf products and the add): saves one pass of the scratch and the add, about 0.2% of
    interpolate. Left.

## Found

- Emerald Rapids at 2^20: the lanes' memory-bound passes take a large share (perf, evaluate/20:
  `pointwise_twice` 9.7%, product_tree's `pointwise2` 6.5%; newton/20 its `combine_product` in
  `newton_lanes` 7.8%). Past L2 a pointwise product costs 0.40-0.44 ns per word there against
  0.12-0.15 in L2 (`leaf3.cpp`); the other machines 1.3-1.7x. Fewer passes over the lanes (the
  tree build's product pass with its copy: #91 r1 tried its removal and lost on Zen 3) would help
  there most.
- ntt's `Transform::multiply` (WideBottom on Zen 5): 0.39 ns per word at 2^12-2^16, 0.44 at 2^17,
  0.50 at 2^20 (`leaf3.cpp`), while product_tree's zmm leaf products, the same pairs of leaves
  without the transform's stage, stay at 0.41-0.43 to 2^20: WideBottom's loss past the caches
  (#98 r1, not understood there) is its own. On Neoverse V2 its leaf products (1.33) are slower
  than the by-lane kernel here (1.25): `umlal` by element may help ntt's too (#98, item 1).
