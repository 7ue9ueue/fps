# #136 r1: faster evaluate, interpolate and Newton basis on every target

2026-10-16, claude (issue #136, round 1, target all). Base main e88390e. GCC 15.2 (the pinned
image) for base and new alike; each target's flags. Explorations (phases, the division's parts,
kernels, crossovers, the cost-model grid and fit, the lc-opt harness with an output check, the
fused-halving attempt as a diff) in `~/Documents/hpc_library-explore/evaluation/issue136/` and
`~/explore/hpc_library/evaluation/` on the VMs.

## Kept

- **Root division** (`root_window`): `fps::series::inverse` (in the stack) instead of the local
  Newton loop (separate forwards, products and inverses, the recurrence by `%` below 32), and the
  three products as `ntt::Transform::cyclic_product` from sources of at most L / 2 words, in the
  output itself; the tree's T(Q) times 2^32 taken out in r's pass (`subtract_scaled`). Zen 3
  (`lc-bench`), K = 2^17, us: inverse to 2^16 898 -> 720; a cyclic product 355 against 370 for
  forward (120), leaf products (127) and inverse (125); the division 2.28 -> 1.96 ms. Alone
  (3 rounds, lc-bench): gated geomean 0.962; evaluate/17 0.970, /20 0.976, interpolate/17 0.979,
  newton/17 0.969; 0.89-0.95 at 2^8 to 2^12, where the old loop's fixed costs showed.
- **Top tree's leaves by transposes** (`lanes_to_rows` into rows of the lane root, m8 words more
  memory) instead of StandardTree's strided loads (stride 8): M4, 2^17 points, 92 -> 43 us; 2^20
  287 -> 206 us.
- **Newton basis, the top tree's rightmost path from known windows** (lc-opt's): the windows on
  it are prefixes of the root's, so those right children's states are forwards of known
  coefficients (no inverse_upper) and lane 7's row is the root window's first C words (no
  inverse). M4: newton_tree 2^17 about 1.5% faster.
- **Unit terms in the bases**: every product in a base is of factors 1 - a x, with constant term
  1 (kR in Montgomery form), so the terms by it are added after the Montgomery step instead of
  multiplied: `multiply_units` (product_tree, also the tree's own bases through `kUnitTerms`;
  product_tree/notes/136-r1.md), `middle_lanes<L, true>`, `cross_units` (whose two sums also share
  one Montgomery step when they have at most 9 products together). Kernels alone, ns per call,
  Zen 3 / M4 (clang): multiply 1x1 9.0 -> 7.1 / 17.6 -> 8.0, 2x2 11.7 -> 12.0 / 26.7 -> 7.7, 4x4
  29.8 -> 30.6 / 51 -> 20, 8x8 83 -> 76 / 42 -> 44; middle 8+8 85 -> 73 / 30 -> 34. Phases on
  Zen 3 with the above: newton_tree 10.21 -> 9.62 ms, interpolate pass 9.94 -> 9.70.
- **x86 middle products and cross sums by loads**: GCC spills the unrolled kernels' operand arrays
  (k + count + k + 1 vectors of two halves: 50 ymm for 8 + 8); `middle_blocks` (blocks of 2
  outputs, operands loaded and shifted per term) and `cross_loads` (one output at a time) do not
  keep them. Zen 3, a base of 16 slots, products + descent, ns, 3 runs: middle_lanes 602-615,
  with units 606-615, blocks 567-584, blocks with units 541-551 (kept); the unit term as w 2^32
  in the sum (a shift, not a product) 554-566; blocks of 4 583-593. Interpolation's base sums
  510 -> 448 ns.
- **Batch inversion in 4 chains** (`divide_slots`): the base's 16 vectors' prefix products were
  one chain of 16 Montgomery steps before the power and one after (latency, not throughput, is
  the cost); 4 interleaved chains, lazy (< 2P) steps and a lazy power. Zen 3, per base: 364 ->
  314 ns.
- **Direct paths in vectors**: interpolation below kDirectInterpolate: M = prod (x - a_i) in
  vectors over the coefficients, M'(a_i) by `horner`, then f = sum w_i M / (x - a_i) by synthetic
  divisions on vectors of points (4 chains); the Newton basis below kDirectNewton: its n - 1
  synthetic divisions in a systolic array (`newton_small`: lane l of a block runs division k0 +
  l one coefficient behind lane l - 1, whose last result it takes by a lane shift; 4 vectors, so
  32 divisions per pass on AVX2). Zen 3, us, old scalar / new / tree: interpolate m = 64 50.3 /
  5.9 / 14.6, 96 108 / 11.3 / 14.6, 128 190 / 18.7 / 14.6; Newton n = 64 7.8 / 0.88 / 9.0, 128
  33.6 / 2.8 / 9.1, 256 138 / 9.9 / 10.0. Newton vectors on M4 at n = 128: 1 9.8, 2 5.7, 4 3.4,
  8 3.5 us (4 kept). Cutoffs measured per machine (`cross.cpp`, steps of 16: the last size where
  the direct path wins before the tree wins for good): Zen 3 interpolate 96 / Newton 352, Zen 5
  128 / 400, Emerald Rapids 128 / 400, Neoverse V2 96 / 192 (M4: about 100 / 184; Emerald Rapids
  and V2 on `lc-intel-test` and `lc-arm-test`, the same CPUs, while their timing machines were
  taken). The trees' costs jump past 256 and 512 points (padding to the next lane length), so the
  direct paths win again just above those (Zen 3: Newton at 272-352, while the tree was 2% faster
  at 248-256; V2 at 272-288).
- **Horner / tree cost model refit** (`kEvaluateCosts`): the old constants were fitted to n or m
  = 4096 against 8 and misjudged n = m: on the new grid (`grid.cpp`, n, m in 8 .. 16384, `fit.py`:
  relative least squares where Horner and the tree are within 4x) the old ones picked the slower
  path at 13 points on Zen 3 (worst 1.53x; n = m = 256: Horner 14.0 us, tree 11.1) and 19 on Zen 5
  (worst 1.67x at n = 2048, m = 192; all of n = 512 with m >= 256); the fit at 5 (worst 1.06x)
  and 4 (worst 1.27x at n = 384, m = 768; two of the four within 2%); Emerald Rapids 21 wrong
  (worst 1.77x) -> 3 (1.08x), Neoverse V2 19 (1.68x) -> 4 (1.09x) (test machines, as above; on
  `lc-intel` itself, later, the old constants 22 wrong (1.76x), these 4 (1.11x), its own fit 3).
  {horner, fixed, point, coefficient}: Zen 3 {0.21, 4840, 2.90, 0.860}, Zen 5 {0.064, 3739,
  1.59, 0.391}, Emerald Rapids {0.1065, 3621, 2.353, 0.746}, V2 {0.2286, 4247, 4.338, 1.300}.

## Tried, not kept

- **The halving fused with the next products** (the issue's lead, lc-opt's estimate 0.08 ms):
  each node got its children's products instead of its state; the halving's forward of L / 2
  went into a second buffer with `Transform::forward_hooked`, whose hook formed s = P_lo -+ T and
  wrote s Q_r and s Q_l (or s, the Newton basis) on each run of leaves, in cache; the roots'
  forwards the same. No combine pass, no product passes. Zen 3, one halving step (copy,
  inverse_upper, forward, combine, two products) against the fused one, ns: 128 words 86 / 95, 256
  172 / 188, 512 367 / 351, 4096 3575 / 3493, 2^14 16652 / 16583, 2^16 78610 / 78963, 2^18 349431 /
  349525; the descent at 2^17 4.87 -> 4.93-4.95 ms (+1.3%); M4 equal. The passes are bound by
  their Montgomery products, not by memory (two products and a subtraction: 0.38 ns per word on
  Zen 3 at 2^16 words), and the hooked forward is 4-7% slower than the in-place one from 2^15
  words. Diff: `issue136/attempts/fused_halving_and_division.diff`.
- **The blocked division** (series' `divide`, lc-opt's log: 4 blocks of K / 4, transforms of K / 2,
  Toeplitz sharing) for the root: Zen 3, K = 2^17, 1952 us against Karp and Markstein's 1960 (same
  outputs); M4 1932 / 2012. Equal on the target that matters; not kept.
- **Transform::multiply (exact leaf products, ntt) for the top tree's products** instead of
  product_tree's `leaf_products` (Montgomery), ns per word alone: Zen 3 0.94-0.97 / 0.82-0.85,
  Zen 5 0.59-0.61 / 0.51-0.52, Emerald Rapids 0.83-0.87 / 0.72-0.78, Neoverse V2 1.33 / 1.47, M4
  0.72 / 0.67. Only V2 gains (9%, about 0.6% of evaluate there); not done. Pointwise products: Zen
  3 0.18-0.21, Zen 5 0.06-0.12, Emerald Rapids 0.13-0.18 (0.36-0.40 past L2), V2 0.25, M4 0.15:
  a leaf product costs 4.4-8x a pointwise one. perf at 2^17
  (evaluate, before this round): product_tree's leaf_products 9.1% on Emerald Rapids, 10.2% on
  Neoverse V2, the largest kernel after the transforms (evidence put on #98).
- Not tried, by arithmetic:
  - Bases of 32 slots (a lead): per 32 slots on Zen 3 the descent saves a halving at 512 words
    (about 330 ns) and the 16-slot level's two products and inverses (150), minus an inverse of
    512 (107): about 370 ns; it adds a 16 + 16 middle level (2 x 17 terms, about 280 ns) and the
    16-slot nodes' products (2 x 8x8, 170): a loss of about 80 ns per 32 slots; the tree build
    loses too (16x16 schoolbook, 240 ns, against a level of 256-word transforms, 135).
  - Keeping the tree's base products for the descent (a lead): 496 words per 16-slot base (2 MB at
    2^17, 16 MB at 2^20) written by the tree and read by the descent, against 200 ns per base to
    recompute (now, with unit terms): about even at 2^17 in L3, a loss at 2^20 from DRAM (32 MB
    of traffic, about 2 ms, against 1.7 ms of products).
  - Interpolation's top leaves to standard transforms in the transform domain (a lead): saves a
    transform of m8 words; its forward twin lost on AVX-512 (notes/104-r1.md).
