# #197 r1: arm square products, the hot loop's address

2026-10-16, claude, branch `agent/small-arm-r1-197` on main a99d165 (GCC 16.2.0 pinned). `lc-arm`
(Neoverse V2, Axion), `-O2 -mcpu=neoverse-v2`. Files, scripts and raw output:
`~/Documents/hpc_library-explore/small/issue197/` (`out/`). Timings of single variants: `loop.cpp`
(one `Schoolbook` in a loop, checked against the model; `run.sh`: binaries interleaved under the
bench lock, median of 7 samples of 20 ms, median of 2-3 repetitions); `bench`: `kernel.py bench
small --base`.

## Cause

Not a worse loop: the place of a loop. On main (whose 16.2 table picks R8 U4 at 192-512 a side),
16.2's `multiply/256x256` and `/512x512` took 7125 and 26850 ns against 6855 and 25779 for 15.2's
`neon.r8.u1` (the variant of 15.2's table there). `neon.r8.u1` itself, 16.2 against 15.2: 128x128
1.050, 256x256 1.068, 512x512 1.081, and 4096x128 0.914, faster (`out/lc-arm/b0-main-162v152.log`).
- Both compilers emit the same 25 instructions for the loop with all R vectors (`terms<0, R>`) in
  `edge` and in `panel` (`full`): 1 `ld1r`, 4 `ldp`, 16 `umlal`/`umlal2`, the pointer ops, `cmp`,
  `b.ne`. Only the addresses differ. `Schoolbook<Neon, 8, P, 1, false, 2>` in `loop.cpp`: 15.2 put
  `edge`'s loop at 32 mod 64 and `panel`'s at 16, 16.2 `edge`'s at 8 and `panel`'s at 0; in the
  small bench binary `edge`'s at 0 (15.2) and 8 (16.2).
- `perf` (512x512, R8 U1, `loop.cpp`): 99% of the cycles in `edge`, on the loop's `umlal`s; 16.2
  runs 2% fewer instructions per call (270903 against 276319) in 8% more cycles (83593 against
  77295; `pstat.sh`).
- Scan: the loop moved through all 16 word offsets mod 64 (`.p2align 6` and n nops before it,
  `-fno-align-loops`; `v/shift`, `out/lc-arm/r2-shift.txt`). At 0 and 32 mod 64: 512x512 25628 and
  25619 ns, 4096x128 50025 and 49425; at each of the other 14: 1.089-1.101 and 1.087-1.101 of
  that. So a loop that does not start at a multiple of 32 bytes costs 9% here (about 0.9 cycles per
  step of about 9.4).
- `-mcpu=neoverse-v2` sets `-falign-loops=32:16` in both GCC versions: GCC pads to 32 only when
  that takes at most 15 bytes, so a loop at 4, 8, 12 or 16 mod 32 stays there. Any change to the
  code before a loop moves it; 15.2 and 16.2 differ in that code (and 15.3, the same as 16.2 in
  #190), so the hot loop's luck changed. With `-falign-loops=32` in both builds: 16.2 at 0.995
  (128x128) and 0.988 (512x512) of 15.2's default build, and 4096x128 0.911 for both.
- Other kernels' compiler-generated loops on arm have the same exposure: #230.
- #37 r3 saw the same with R4 U1 (+17% at 8 or 40 mod 64) and read it as the closing `b.ne`
  starting a 32-byte block; with GCC's alignment off, every start that is not a multiple of 32 is
  slow. Guess: the taken branch to a target inside a 32-byte fetch block costs a cycle per
  iteration.

## Fix: the loop with all R vectors starts at a multiple of 32 bytes

`neon_terms` (Neon only; x86 and scalar code unchanged): every term loop is a loop on pointers
(b' at p .. end, the windows' base one word lower per step; the empty `asm` that keeps the base
opaque is on the pointer itself, so no copy: 24 instructions instead of 25). The loop with all R
vectors gets `asm volatile(".p2align 5" : "+r"(p), "+r"(base) : "r"(end))` right before it: its
pointers are the asm's operands, so they are computed before it and nothing comes between the
padding and the loop. Checked in the objdump of both compilers' builds (R4, R6, R8; `edge` and
`panel`): every such loop at 0 mod 32. The padding (at most 7 `nop`s) runs once per entry, at most
every 17 steps. Unroll U > 1 on Neon: the remainder steps first, then the aligned loop of U steps.

Tried, in order (`loop.cpp`, R8 U1 unless named):
1. `-falign-loops=32` for the whole file: the effect above. Not ours to set (users' flags).
2. `[[gnu::optimize("align-loops=32")]]` on `panel`, `edge`, `one`, `tiny`, `panels` (`v/attr`):
   16.2 0.989-1.007 of 15.2 at 64-512, but 15.2 itself 1.052 at 32x32, 1.023 at 64x64: every loop
   of those functions is padded, and the padding runs at each entry of the short phase loops (61
   more instructions and 30 more cycles per 32x32 call). GCC also documents the attribute as not
   for production. Not kept.
3. `.p2align 5` in an `asm` before the old `for` loop (`v/pre`, first form): GCC put the loop guard
   and three pointer setups after it, and in `panel` a `mov` of the pointer, so the loop sat at 4
   mod 32. Hence the pointer operands.
4. The kept form, R4/R6/R8 U1 (`v/pre`, `v/aln`): 16.2 R8 U1 64x64 to 512x512 0.993-1.004 of 15.2's
   R8 U1 on main (`out/lc-arm/r4-pre.txt`); R4 U1 0.94-0.98 at 16x16-128x128. 15.2 R8 U1 0.994-1.009.
5. Also aligning the phase loops of fewer vectors, the tiny path's and `one`'s (`v/aln`, 16.2):
   phases 512x512 0.989 of (4) but 8x8 and 16x16 1.02-1.05 (the padding at each phase, at most 4
   steps); tiny and `one` no change. Not kept.
6. U: aligned U = 4 loses to U = 1 everywhere: R8 U4 512x512 25822 against 25417 ns, 4096x4 3333
   against 2949; R6 U4 4096x128 53287 against R6 U1 50184 (`out/lc-arm/r5-aln.txt`). Guess: U = 4
   won on arm (#37 r2: 0.95) because it paid the unaligned loop's cycle once per 4 steps. R5 U1 and
   R7 U1 on 18 shapes: never more than 1% faster than the best of R4, R6 and R8 (16x16: R5 72, R4
   70 ns), up to 1.18 slower than R8 at the large ones (`out/lc-arm/r6-new.txt`); not added.

arm candidates: R8 U1, R6 U1 Solo, R4 U1, R6 U1, R6 U1 Solo T4, R8 U1 T4, R4 U1 T4 (were R6 U4, R6 U4
Solo, R4 U1, R8 U4, R8 U1, R6 U4 Solo T4, R8 U1 T4). The table started from main's (each old
candidate replaced by its U = 1 counterpart, `bootstrap.py`), then `tools/tune.py small --target
arm` (commit 275baa0): 6 classes moved (2, 16, 18, 20, 21, 22; class 24 kept R8 U1, 0.8% behind R4
U1 there, within the 1% margin); the fit's term 0.0905 ns (was 0.0899), word 0.404 (0.425), square
0.0258 (0.0383); the NTT times within 3%; 1 of 540 swept shapes sent the slower way (993x32,
1.011x; the compiled table 2, worst 1.080x). `out/tune-arm-1.txt`.
