# #253 r1: products of more than 2^24 small factors, past the lane pairs

2026-10-18, claude (issue #253, round 1, target all). Base main ff90ae6 (timing), rebased onto
57bb479 (#254) and tested again there. GCC 16.2 (the pinned image), each target's flags. Tests on
`lc-amd` (avx2), `lc-arm-test` (arm) and the Mac (arm, scalar); timing on `lc-bench` (Zen 3). Harness (`bench_large.cpp`, not committed) and logs in
`~/Documents/hpc_library-explore/product_tree/issue253/`.

## The fault

With more than 2^24 small factors of degree sum up to 2^25, the lanes' length L is 2^22, and the
driver asked for the lane root's transform at twice its length (`root_doubled`, #91 r2): 16 L =
2^26 words, past ntt's 2^kMaxLog = 2^25. `reserve_transforms(16 L)` threw `std::length_error`.
Behind it, `untwist_factors(T / 8)` with T = 2^23 read `ntt::detail::kRoots[1][21]`, one past
the table (`kRootCount` = 21), for leaves u >= 2^19.

## Kept

1. **The pairs only while 16 L <= 2^kMaxLog** (`detail::pairs_up_to`, thread_local, default
   2^kMaxLog; tests lower it). Past it the standard tree takes the 8 lane products as leaves and
   their forwards, as before #245 and as for lanes shorter than `kPairsLength`. One condition in
   `detail::product`.
2. **`untwist_factors` past `kRoots`**: the roots of order 2^(j + 2) for j >= `kRootCount`
   computed as `power(3, (P - 1) >> (j + 2))` inverted (as evaluation's `kTopRoots`). With item 1
   the driver never asks for more than 2^19 leaves, but the table is right to 2^20 (rho_u's order
   2^23, the most P allows).

## Why not the twisted half

The issue's other option: the upper half of the doubled transform as a twisted transform of 8 L
words (coefficient j of each lane times zeta^j, zeta of order 2L, then a forward of 8 L), as
evaluation's `lanes_forward_upper` since #136 r2. What it could save, measured at the largest size
where the pairs still run (2^24 linear factors, L = 2^21), `lc-bench`, 3 rounds, same binary:
pairs on 936.0 ms, pairs off (`pairs_up_to` = 2^24) 970.6 ms: the pairs save 3.6%. At 2^24 + 8
the twisted form would pay a twist pass over 8 L = 2^25 words more (guess: 10-17 ms at 0.3-0.5
ns per word, about 1% of the 1350 ms product), so it would gain about 2-3% on degrees in (2^24,
2^25], against a second code path in `root_doubled` with its own tests. Not done; a lead below.

## Numbers

`kernel.py bench product_tree --base ff90ae6`, `lc-bench` (EPYC 7B13, Zen 3), avx2, GCC 16.2,
7 rounds, filter `^product(_mixed|@lanes)?/`: geomean over gated cases 0.9935; product/20 0.998
(IQR 0.994-1.003), product_mixed/20 1.001 (0.998-1.002), product/18 0.999. The hot path gains one
comparison; the moves (product/6 0.955) are layout.

2^24 + 8 linear factors (degree 2^24 + 8), `product@over/24` in the harness, 3 rounds: 1350 ms,
80.5 ns per factor (main: throws). 2^24 factors: 936 ms, 55.8 ns per factor.

## Tests

`test_large.cpp`: `untwist_factors(2^20)` at leaves to 2^20 - 1 against rho_u from the bits of u
and against rho_u^8 = w_u (`leaf_weights`); products with `pairs_up_to` lowered from 2^8 to past
the lanes' doubled length (linear factors 136 .. 20000, degrees 2 and 5, mixed with constants and
a large factor) against a sequential product (to 3000 coefficients), random points and the default
path's output; 2^24 + 8 linear factors x - r_i at 3 random points and at 3 of their roots (not
under ASan, to keep its memory and time down: the lowered `pairs_up_to` covers the path). Against
main's headers: the 2^24 + 8 product throws and 42 untwist checks fail. `kernel.py test
product_tree evaluation frontend` on `lc-amd` (avx2), `lc-arm-test` (arm) and the Mac (arm,
scalar); `kernel.py check`.

## Other callers

`fps::product` (include/fps/products.hpp) goes through this driver: fixed with it. evaluation
before #254 did not double the lane root; since #254 (#136 r2) it doubles it and runs twisted
transforms at m8 = 2^25 with its own twist tables (`kTopRoots`), so it does not hit this limit.
roots uses product_tree's kernels only.

## Leads

- The twisted upper half of `root_doubled` at 16 L > 2^kMaxLog (above): about 2-3% on products
  of degree in (2^24, 2^25] (a guess from the 3.6% the pairs save at 2^24).
