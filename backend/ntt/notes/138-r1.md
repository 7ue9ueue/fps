## What went in

`backend/ntt/dft.hpp` (new): `ntt::dft(f, out)`, out[i] = f(w^i) for L = out.size() = 2^6 .. 2^23,
any f.size(), and `ntt::idft(values, out)`, its inverse. `fps::dft` and `fps::idft`
(`include/fps/geometric.hpp`) call them for those L; other L stay on the chirp.

- The order of the values form (common.hpp): word p = 64 g + 8 i + t is leaf v = 8 g + t at
  rho_v z^bitrev3(i). With c of order 2^23, r(k) = c^rev22(k), so rho_v = r(4 v) = w^rev_(lg-3)(v)
  and z = r(2) = w^(L/8): word p is f(w^e) with e = rev_lg(64 g + 8 t + i) = rev6(8 t + i) 2^n +
  rev_n(g), n = lg - 6. Natural order is 64 runs of 2^n words (run 8 i + t at rev6(8 t + i) 2^n),
  word rev_n(g) of a run from block g: a bit reversal of the blocks and a transpose of each.
- dft: f (folded mod x^L - 1 if longer, `ragged::fold` then `add_words`) to the values form in the
  thread's region: `Variant::forward_hooked` reads f from its first level, and its hook runs the
  values form's split (`ValuesFor<Native>::split`) on each run of leaves in cache, as
  `forward(f, Form::values)` does; then `permute` into out. idft: `permute<kInverse>` into the
  region, then the variant's values-form inverse (`Variant::inverse(w, w, out, ...)`, as
  `Transform::inverse(F, work, out, Form::values)`), whose scale 2^-lg is the 1 / L; its top level
  writes out. Tables: `detail::tables<Variant>` (compile-time to 2^16, then the thread's), the
  value rows built once per length (`value_rows`, call_once). Same variants as the transforms:
  `SelectedForward`, `SelectedInverse`. f copied into the work array and the values form in place
  (`forward_values`) instead of `forward_hooked`: 0.98-1.04 of the time (first runs, all four).
- `permute` (simd struct S, W lanes): tiles of R blocks g = rev_rho(r) 2^(n - rho) + m (m =
  rev(c) for tile c), whose words are words [R c, R c + R) of every run: per W x W piece, W row
  loads from W blocks, `factorials::transpose`, W stores to W runs. R = 64 from 2^14, 16 below;
  from 2^14 both sides through stack buffers (40 KiB): the tile's blocks copied whole, and, when
  the natural side is not aligned to the runs' chunks, the runs too, written (read) a whole aligned
  line at a time with the partial line carried to the next tile. Lengths with R < W run on Scalar
  (2^6-2^9 on 16 lanes).

## Attempts (bench_dft.cpp's `@` cases and exploration variants; whole ops unless marked)

The values form's forward alone (`dft@forward`) costs 1.2-1.6x `to_values` (the frontend's
yardstick, a leaf-form forward of L / 2 words; the split adds 0.4 ns per word on Zen 3, notes.md),
so dft cannot reach 1.5x `to_values` at most lengths whatever the permutation; the permutation
was the part to get near a copy's cost.

- v1: R = 16 blocks, from 2^14 the tile's blocks copied whole into a stack buffer (dft) or out of it
  (idft); stores straight to out. lc-bench, first run: dft 1.74-1.93x `to_values` at 2^12-2^22,
  the permutation 0.12-0.50 ns per word (a copy: 0.04-0.17). Without the buffer, rows of a block
  are half lines (avx2) or quarters (neon) read once per piece: 0.40-0.48 ns per word at
  2^16-2^20, 1.1-1.3x the buffered. R = 64 without the buffer: 2.3-2.7x slower in the whole op on
  Zen 3 at 2^18-2^20 (6.0 ms against 2.3 at 2^20). Software prefetch of the next tile's blocks (1
  or 2 tiles ahead): 0.95-1.06 of no prefetch on all four, dropped.
- Alignment of the natural side (permutation alone, out 64-byte aligned against 16 bytes past a
  line, as malloc gives std::vector): Zen 5 dft R = 64 2^20 0.220 against 0.383 ms, 2^22 1.05
  against 1.57 ms; R = 16 2^20 0.448 against 0.632; idft (loads) 0.21 against 0.28. A run's chunk
  then shares its first and last lines with the next tile's, all 64 runs 2^n words apart in the
  same L1 set: the half-written lines leave L1 before the next tile completes them.
- v2: the h loop (a line of W blocks' rows in W x W pieces back to back), and the natural side
  through a buffer when it is not aligned: chunks go out shifted to the line boundary, the last a
  words kept for the next tile (a: the side's words past a line), so every line of out is written
  once, whole. Zen 5 permutation at 2^16-2^20: 0.165-0.20 ns per word for either alignment
  (0.38-0.56 before). But with the values side read straight from the work array, the whole op on
  Zen 3 at 2^18 ran 3.6x `to_values` (1.29 ms against v1's 0.53): rows 2^lg / 64 words apart, the
  work array just written by the forward (guess: L2 sets, 8 ways, 16 rows a set; the permutation
  alone on a fresh array ran 0.35 ns per word).
- v3 (in): both buffers from 2^14, R = 64 from 2^14 (16 below), each optional (template
  arguments, `Order`). In the official runs below, `@direct` (values side not buffered) ran
  1.4-3.2x the default on Zen 3 and 1.8-2.7x on Zen 5 at 2^18-2^20, and 0.90-0.95 of it on Emerald
  Rapids at 2^15-2^18 (dft; the idft 0.97-1.07): buffered everywhere, as no rule from these four
  machines would be safer. `@r16` (tiles of 16 blocks) up to 1.4x the default (Zen 5 2^16, Emerald
  Rapids 2^19-2^21).

## Found

- The values form's split costs about as much as the permutation: 19 Shoup products and two
  transposes per 64 words, 0.2-0.4x of `to_values` here. Three radix-2 levels more in the
  transform's bottom stage would be 12 products per 64 words, no transposes (guess; the asm
  bottoms are generated). It would cut dft and `Layout::pointwise` alike.
- `fps::idft`'s canonical check is now 4-12% of the kernel's time (one more pass over the values,
  0.1-0.19 ns per word: `idft@check` in `bench_api.cpp`), and `fps::dft`'s zero-filled
  `std::vector` 1-14%. The permutation reads every value: a check there (a subtract and a min per
  vector) would save the pass; the idft's output is the API's new polynomial, so writing it before
  the throw is harmless.
- `test_dft.cpp` includes `backend/chirp` (its reference), so a chirp change now selects ntt in the
  gate (only that test's preprocessed text changes, so `--base` rebuilds only it).
- Rows of blocks 2^lg / R words apart, read straight after a transform wrote them, ran up to 3.2x
  slower on Zen 3 and 2.7x on Zen 5 than through a buffer of whole blocks (`@direct`): other passes
  with that access may pay it too.
- `lc-amd` powered off in the middle of a test run (a container running); `vm.py` restarted it.
