## Why: Zen 5 decodes about 3 instructions per cycle past its op cache

`opc.cpp` (`lc-zen5-test`, one core): a loop of N straight-line instructions that could run 8 per
cycle (4 zmm vpaddd and 4 scalar adds, 5 bytes on average): 7.96 per cycle up to N = 5120, 6.5 at
5376, 6.0 at 5632, 3.2 from 5888. With 7-byte (vpaddd with a disp32 memory operand) or 5.6-byte
instructions that run 4 per cycle: 4.0 up to 5888, 2.9 from 6144. So the op cache holds about 5.9K
instructions whatever their length, and past it a thread gets about 3 per cycle (16-20 bytes; Zen
5 gives a thread one of its two decode clusters). The kernels run 4-5 per cycle from the op cache.

`trace.cpp` single-steps one call under ptrace and records every instruction address (distinct
instructions per call; with main's table / with intrinsics<Avx512> where it has avx512):

| case | main | intrinsics | | case | main | intrinsics |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| forward/10 | 2027 | 1019 | | multiply/12 | 5682 | 4276 |
| inverse/10 | 1476 | 1260 | | multiply/14 | 6814 | 5308 |
| multiply/10 | 4602 | 3345 | | evaluate/10 | 15114 | 12943 |
| evaluate_geometric/8 | 3919 | 3260 | | sqrt/11 | 11318 | 10472 |
| square/12 | 5384 | 4044 | | inverse/11 (series) | 11067 | 8648 |

The asm statements are big where the intrinsics loop (bottom forward 4 pairs: 393 instructions, the
intrinsics' WideBottom::forward loop about 150; forward groups of two columns, 4 per statement:
469 + 435 for group 0's statement), and the group loops were inlined two and three times
(`forward_group<4>`, `forward_groups`, `inverse_group`, `inverse_groups` each held a copy):
`multiply/14` ran 2-3 copies of each. The composite workloads (11-15K instructions, 70-80 KB per
call) are far past the op cache and the 32 KiB L1I whatever the variant, so they run largely at
the decoders' pace, where the asm's extra distinct instructions cost and its scheduling gains
little. evaluate/10 by function group: product_tree and evaluation 7354 instructions, the zmm asm
variant 6284 (intrinsics: 3880), ymm intrinsics 587, avx2 466. A cold-code harness (`cold.cpp`:
each call after 48 KiB of straight-line code) ran the asm forward/9-10 at 0.80-1.06 of the
intrinsics' time against 0.82-0.85 warm (noisy: the TSC ticks in steps of 27).

## Round 1's leads (kernels on L1 data, `kern.cpp`, asm / intrinsics, 7 rounds)

- **Scaled inverse top groups** (`inverse_scaled`, `inverse_scaled_identity`: intrinsics.hpp's
  `inverse_group_scaled`, 5 products per column; 261 and 231 instructions, 4 columns per
  iteration, twiddles s, s x, s y, s z, x in registers): 0.88-0.95 at quarters of 128-2048 words.
  Kept. Same binary, run-time switch, two builds: the inverse 0.3-3% faster at 2^10-2^16,
  products and squares at 2^11 4-7% faster, at 2^10 2-6% slower.
- **First levels from other memory** (`forward_from_in2`, `forward_identity_from_in2`: the columns
  read from a factor in the caller's memory, written to the work array; odd words by shifts, so
  nothing is read past the source): 0.87-0.98 on L1 data. Kept for inputs < 2P (the subtrees'
  first levels under a radix-2 top). Same binary, four builds: f * g and product/10 0.2-2.6%
  faster in three, product/10 11% slower in one (below). The form for canonical inputs
  (`forward_identity_from_in1`, the radix-4 top at odd lg; 0.94-1.03 on L1 data) made middle
  products at 2^9 slower: bench middle_product/9 1.05-1.09 in five runs, same binary 3.3%. Dropped.
- **Inverse groups of two columns** (`inverse_groups4`, group 0 by its table twiddles 1, 1, r(1),
  so no `_first` statement): 0.86-0.94 on L1 data; same binary: inverse 1-3% faster, products at
  2^10-2^11 2.8-3.8% slower, evaluate/10, newton/10, series inverse/11, f * g at 2^10 0.5-1.8%
  slower. Across binaries with it products at 2^14-2^21 ran 4-6.5% slower (n3), 1.00 without (n2).
  Not kept (round 1 saw the same at 2^10).
- **Code layout moves single ops by up to 10%**: the same source with the same run-time switches,
  built twice, ran product/10 1119 and 1026 ns with all hooks on, 995 and 1033 ns with the first
  levels off. Every decision above rests on same-binary switches or several builds.

## Open

- Composite workloads at 2^10 are decode-bound (11-15K instructions per call); the asm path adds
  15-35% distinct instructions to the ntt's share. Smaller asm (loops of fewer instructions per
  call at short lengths) without losing the hot speed is the remaining lever; the lean sets above
  lost 5-25% on the ops alone.
- Products at 2^12-2^16 run 5.6-5.7K distinct instructions per call, at the op cache's size; the
  product bottom (`multiply_first/both/last`, 1161 instructions) is the largest piece. A smaller
  bottom stage (or no `_last`: `both` with a dummy next pair) would leave room.
- Why inverse groups of two columns slow products (not code size alone: 477 instructions), and
  middle products at 2^9-2^10 run 5-9% slower since the first levels from memory.
- The tuned table was not remeasured (`tools/tune.py ntt --target zen5`); intrinsics<Avx512> keeps
  inverse/7 and multiply/8, avx2 the 2^6-2^7 entries.
