## The move

lc-opt's `factor.hpp`, `kernel.hpp`, `field.hpp`, `poly.hpp` (odd p < 2^30, AVX2 only, Poly of
128 words on the stack, degree <= 100) became `backend/factor/` for P = 998244353 only (no p = 2,
no small-p or p-th-root paths: deg f < P makes f' = 0 only for constants):

- The dense kernels as templates on `backend/simd` structs (sums in 64-bit lanes: `EvenOdd` x86,
  `Halves` Neon, `Single` scalar), so scalar, sse, avx2, avx512, zen5 and arm all run it; the ymm
  register path for moduli of degree <= 8 kept for structs of 8 lanes or more under AVX2.
- P a constant: folds every 17 products (lc-opt: 14), constants folded by the compiler.
- Any degree up to `kMaxDegree` = 4096: polynomials from a pool of buffers sized for the input,
  zeroed on return (only the words below n); scratch per factorizer.
- API `fps::factorization::factor(span) -> vector<Factor>` sorted (degree, then coefficients);
  f = 0 throws `invalid_argument`, deg > 4096 `length_error`.

First same-run comparison on `lc-bench` (port only, commit 43e7f6b, 3 rounds, outputs equal):
geomean fps / lc-opt 0.902 (0.82-0.97 per case).

## Attempts (all on top of each other, lc-bench unless said)

| step | geomean fps / lc-opt |

- `combine_rows` (several rows per pass over the columns). Variants, 8 rows x m x m, products per
  ns (`variants.cpp`, m = 100; 64, 128, 256 alike):

  | struct | R4V1 | R2V2 | R8V1 | R4V2 | odd words by a load at +1 (R4V1) |

  | Emerald Rapids avx512 | 21.9 | 21.2 | **23.2** | 23.0 | 22.5 |

  Kept: R4V1, R8V1 on AVX-512 (32 registers). The odd words loaded one word on (no `vpsrlq`): no
  gain (logged, not kept). Single-row `combine` at m = 100: 11.6 (Zen 3), 17.3 (Emerald Rapids),
  39.2 (Zen 5), 8.4 (Neoverse V2).
- Krylov at m = 100 (Zen 3): 83 -> 67 us; at m = 256: 1654 -> 998 us.
- Elimination parts at m = 100 (`elim.cpp`, TSC ticks, Zen 3): work columns 18%, pivot rows 17%,
  the update's coefficients (scalar products, transposed) 16%, the update 42%. The coefficients are
  now the panel columns scaled in place (one vector product per word) and read with a step along s:
  50 -> 42 us at m = 100. Column blocks of 8 / 16 / 32 (`elim2.cpp`, M4 Pro): 16 best at every size
  (100 x 100: 38.1 / 34.9 / 41.3 us; 256 x 256: 516 / 446 / 456 us); kept 16.
- `times_x` chain (multiplication matrices, reduction tables): copy then unaligned load at a - 1
  waited on stores; aligned loads shifted in registers 46 -> 40 ns per column (m = 100, Zen 3);
  the top word from the register, and its vector first: 37 ns. Now throughput-bound (27
  instructions per vector of 8 words); a Shoup product on a precomputed g would cut maybe a
  third (not done: about 3% of a factorization).
- `#pragma GCC novector` (#183) only on scalar sums: on Neon it cost `combine_rows` 15% (8.7 vs
  10.3 products per ns).
- Root finding: Cantor-Zassenhaus took about 1.4 us per root (29 squarings at every node, and a
  new shift when a node does not split: 1 in 4 at degree 3). With the 2-power characters
  (notes.md, Design), `find_roots` on d distinct linear factors (Zen 3, `roots.cpp`):

  | d | 8 | 16 | 32 | 44 | 64 | 128 |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | C-Z (us) | 7.2 | 17.7 | 43.4 | 64.8 | 106.2 | 291.4 |
  | 2-power (us) | 5.6 | 12.8 | 30.8 | 47.7 | 79.0 | 233.3 |

  all_linear100 against lc-opt 0.80 -> 0.64. The degree-2 leaves (Cipolla) are now about a
  quarter of the time at d = 8.
- Kept factorizer: a fresh one per call allocated every pool buffer again, and the stored powers
  x^((P-1)/2^j) added up to 17 more: random16 11.3 -> 12.6 us; with a thread-local factorizer
  (degree <= 256) 10.1 us.
- split() for 44 quadratics (m = 88, Zen 3, `split.cpp`), us: powers of v 26.7 -> 27.6, mu 11.1,
  roots of mu 68.5 -> 53.0, groups 37.0 -> 36.4.
- Column tiles of 256 in `combine` and `combine_rows`: degree 4096 took 28.3 s on Zen 3 against
  1.46 s at 2048 (19x for twice the degree): each output vector read one 4 KiB page per column, and
  4096 columns miss the L2 TLB. See Large degrees.

## Large degrees

`large.cpp`: random f, s per call (median of 3 after a warm-up; the product of the factors checked
equal to monic f). Degree 4096 is `kMaxDegree`, the largest that runs.

| degree | Zen 3 | Emerald Rapids | Zen 5 | Neoverse V2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1024 | 0.144 | 0.148 | 0.040 | ARML1 |
| 2048 | 1.11 | 1.15 | 0.353 | ARML2 |
| 4096 | 17.3 | 9.0 | 5.46 | ARML4 |

Before the column tiles: 0.157, 1.46, 28.3 s (Zen 3), 0.156, 1.26, 14.5 (Emerald Rapids), 0.041,
0.46, 6.08 (Zen 5). From 2048 to 4096 the time still grows 9-16x, not 8x: the 64 MiB matrices
stream from memory once per 8 Krylov rows and per 16 elimination columns (a GEMM-like blocking,
more rows per pass over L2-sized tiles, is the next step there). Degree 2^20 does not run (length
error): the matrices alone would be 4 TiB; notes.md, Limits, has what it would take.
