## Where the time went (main, d708598)

Past degree 1024 the Krylov rows (m mat-vecs of m^2 products; each block of 8 rows streams the m x
m multiplication matrix) were two thirds to nine tenths of the time, the elimination most of the
rest, both at 4-10 products per ns on Zen 3 (14-18 in cache). Random f of degree 4096 on Zen 3:
16.9 s, of it Krylov 14.3 and elimination 2.8. At degree 88-100 the dense kernels are near their
rate; a profile of 44 quadratics (perf, Emerald Rapids, after changes 1-4 below) had
`rows_group` 23%, `Ring::gcd` 12.7%, `combine_rest` (single-row combines) 11%,
`Ring::divide_words` 10.9%, `multiply` 6.9%, `times_x_chain` 4.7%, `multiply_small` 3.6%,
`Ring::sqrt` (Cipolla) 3.3%.

## Changes

1. Products modulo g by NTT (`large.hpp`, new). `Products` keeps the leaf forms (2H words, H =
   bit_ceil(m)) of x^(H-m+1) mu, mu = floor(x^(2m-1) / g), and of -g mod (x^H - 1). Barrett for
   polynomials is exact: for deg A <= 2m - 1, floor(A / g) = floor((A div x^m) mu / x^(m-1)) (with
   x^(2m-1) = mu g + rho the rest has negative degree), one cyclic product at 2H whose words [H,
   H + m) are the quotient (no wrap); A - floor(A / g) g has degree < m <= H, so it is the inverse
   at H of A's leaf form there (the first half of its leaf form at 2H) minus the quotient's times
   g's (`inverse_product`, 2 terms). `Ring::multiply_mod` on such a modulus: the product at 2H, its
   words [m, 2m - 1) by an inverse with an offset, the quotient, the remainder, a pass of 2^-32
   (Montgomery words).
2. The Krylov rows by NTT. For a fixed factor r, Shoup's precomputed quotient holds for polynomials:
   floor(q r / g) = floor(q r' / x^m), r' = floor(r x^m / g) (from mu, one cyclic product).
   `Multiplier::step`: a cyclic product of q by x^(H-m) r' at 2H (the quotient), q's transform at
   H, the remainder: about 3.5 transforms of 2H words a row. `Powers`: r .. r^8 and their r'_t once
   (7 products, 8 cyclic products), then per block of 8 rows the first row's transform at 2H once
   and per row an `inverse_product` at 2H (the quotient) and the remainder at H: about 2.1 a row
   (Krylov at 2048 on the M4: 33 -> 26 ms against `Multiplier`). Rows are Montgomery words and r
   canonical, so they stay Montgomery. `power_rows` serves the Berlekamp rows and the split's
   powers of v (`Powers` from 64 rows). Such moduli build no table and no multiplication matrix:
   one m x m matrix in all (64 MiB at 4096, was 2 x 64).

5. `Ring::gcd`: a degree-1 quotient (the usual case) in one pass, B1^2 a - B1 A1 x b - (B1 A0 - A1
   B0) b (3 products per word, one Montgomery step: backend/gcd's step), and every pass
   (`lincomb3`, `lincomb`) from the top vector down, so the next step's factors (its top words)
   are ready before the pass ends. `gcdbench.cpp`, M4, degrees n and n - 1: n = 44 1.74 -> 1.03
   us, 88 3.75 -> 2.77 (the one pass alone 3.29, then top-down), 256 16.0 -> 9.3.
6. `Ring::divide_words`: quotient words in blocks of 16, each block's words by a scalar triangular
   solve on its top words (one fold per word), the words below by one `combine` of the block's 16
   shifts of d (the axpy per quotient word waited on its own last store). M4, 2n by n words: n = 88
   2.45 -> 1.39 us, 256 15.3 -> 6.6 us; Zen 3 n = 88 2.43 us before.
7. Root finding: quadratic nodes in scalars (`quadratic_roots`): w^(2^t) mod Q = alpha_t x + beta_t
   by (alpha x + beta)^2 = alpha (2 beta - alpha b) x + beta^2 - alpha^2 c0; at level j, alpha = 0
   sends both roots down a level (beta = +-c), else they part, one root (c - beta) / alpha (or with
   -c), the other -b minus it: one inverse instead of Cipolla's square root (kept for roots whose
   values agree to all k bits). w_(j+1)^low by a product over the set bits of low, not a power.
   `power_of_x` starts from x^prefix (the longest leading bits of e worth at most 2m - 2: a word or
   a row of the table).

## Tried and dropped

- The roots' chain at the root: w^(2^t) mod mu for t < k once, every node one remainder of it in
  place of k - j - 1 squarings modulo itself (and its table). M4: 44 values 41.8 -> 40.1 us, 128
  values 167 -> 216 us (a degree-127 polynomial reduced by nodes of degree 2-8: long quotients by
  short divisors). Reverted.
- Quadratic leaves from that chain (one remainder per level): roots of mu 37.7 -> 44.6 us (M4). The
  scalar squarings of change 7 instead: 37.4.
- `half_gcd::gcd` (backend/gcd) as `Ring::gcd`: on Zen 3 1.3-1.5x faster at degrees 32-64, slower at
  88 (4.49 against 3.20 us: its jumps start past 64), equal at 128. After change 5, on the M4,
  Ring::gcd is 6% slower at 44, 4% at 88, 21% at 64, 13% faster at 256. Not used.
- Blocks of 64 or 128 columns below 1536 and tiles of 512 words: no better than 16-32 columns and
  whole rows or 1024-word tiles anywhere (table above).

## Found

- The elimination is 80-90% of the time from degree 2048 on, at the `combine_rows` rate; a faster
  update kernel (the R x V variants were measured in #123 r1) or fewer products (another method)
  is what is left there. At 4096 Emerald Rapids gains nothing on Berlekamp's matrix (1.28 -> 1.29
  s) though random matrices gained 15%: worth a look (memory? the gather?).
- `kFactorKernelMax` (`include/fps/algebra.hpp`, frontend) should become 4096: the kernel is
  2.9-4.7x faster than the frontend path at 2^12 now (table above).
- `kMaxDegree` = 4096 could go to 8192 (256 MiB of matrix, about 8x the time of 4096: 13-22 s on
  Zen 3, Emerald Rapids, Neoverse V2, 5 s on Zen 5), still 2-3x faster than the frontend path
  there by extrapolation; the memory is the question (#201 is the road past it).
- `backend/factor/bench.cpp`'s `<case>@frontend` cases time the kernel through `fps::factor` since
  #209 (degree <= 2048), not the frontend path; `frontend/bench_algebra.cpp`'s `factor@frontend`
  are the ones that time `factor_by_splitting`.
- Small degrees: x^((P-1)/2) by squarings is 15-25% at m = 88-100 (a squaring kernel with half the
  products, or Karatsuba on the square, would cut a fifth of it); the split's per-node work
  (gcd, an inverse per gcd for the monic result, divisions) is most of "groups" and "roots of mu"
  for many factors.
