1. Each step's factors in one product level (x = alpha^2, y = -alpha beta, z = beta d1 - alpha c1,
   no factor 2^32: remainders and rows only matter up to constants), the next remainder's top two
   words in scalar from the scalars and words 3-4 of the last step's vectors (`combine3_word`), and
   the vectors top-down. Alone: no change. The chain was not the limit: a build without the
   memory reads in the chain (wrong words, same steps) ran as fast.
2. `perf stat` on `lc-intel`: 3.4M `ld_blocks.store_forward` in 0.09 s. GCC copied the rows' sizes
   (an array in a struct behind a pointer, `Rows::rotate`) with 16-byte moves right after the
   8-byte stores of the step, so every step's loads waited for those stores to retire. The state
   (pointers, sizes, degrees) in locals of `steps()`; the old code had the same pattern
   (`row_size[3][2]` swapped in a loop).
3. The odd words of X v are the even words of v, so a step takes 2 shifts, not 3. The words one
   place down by unaligned loads: lane shifts of aligned loads (valignd, ext, vperm2i128 +
   vpalignr), which store-to-load forwarding serves whole, ran 0.99-1.07x their time.
4. Two steps per pass: e (3 products) and f = (a0 c + a1 X c + b0 d + b1 X d + b2 X^2 d) 2^-32 (5
   products, factors from the two steps' by Montgomery products) from c and d, so a pass's
   vectors wait on the last pass's stores once per two steps. Slower everywhere: 8 products
   per two steps for 6 (more uops on the throughput-bound machines), and the pass waits for the
   second step's factors, whose scalars need words 2-5 of the last pass's vectors (chain about 37
   cycles per step by count, as before). Deleted.

Where the time goes, Zen 5, k <= 64 (builds with parts switched off): with fixed factors (no scalar
chain) and no rows, 13 ns per step (53 cycles) at k = 16 to 64, as with the chain: each step's
vectors read the last step's stores, a chain of a load, 4 products deep and a join per step. Zen 3:
57 cycles. At k = 64 the rows and remainders together are 8 zmm vectors per step on Zen 5 (about
44 cycles of throughput), 18 ymm on Zen 3 (6.3 cycles each, from the k = 64 -> 128 slope).

Need masks at the leaves: a column no entry in `need` has keeps its coefficient 0 (for at0), and
the last step's d row is skipped unless row 1 is wanted. inverse_mod's top (need 0x2) at 2^6 is one
such leaf.

gcd's tail (whole remainders, no rows) runs the same `steps()`: gcd/6 0.64-0.72.

## The products without the 2^32 bookkeeping

The matrices' transforms were kept times 2^32 (a `to_montgomery` pass per fold and doubling, and a
second reduction of every combine product) so that products came out unscaled. A product of two
values forms by Montgomery is the product times 2^-32, and a matrix only matters up to a constant
as long as its apply and its combine read the same entries: the apply gives 2^-32 R1 (a, b), the
combine 2^-32 R2 R1, coefficient 0 (at0, the wrap fixes) by the same Montgomery reduction. Both
passes and the reduction gone. Steps 1-3 above with this (kernel bench, 3 rounds, new/base,
geomean; 2^20 gcd / inverse_mod): Zen 3 0.897 (0.936 / 0.937), Emerald Rapids 0.948 (0.973 /
0.969), Zen 5 0.867 (0.928 / 0.932), Neoverse V2 0.898 (0.943 / 0.942).

## The values form on zmm (backend/ntt, notes/157-r1.md there)

`Values<Avx512Vl>::split` 20%, `join` 13.5% (+ 2.7% `Values<Avx2>::join` from the avx2 variant),

6%. The values form's split and join ran on ymm (8 lanes) on AVX-512, adding 0.5-0.9 ns per word
to transforms of 0.4-1.0. `intrinsics::WideValues`: two blocks per step on zmm. Values transforms
0.66-0.93 (Emerald Rapids), 0.72-0.82 (Zen 5) of their time; gcd Emerald Rapids 0.948 -> 0.856,
Zen 5 0.867 -> 0.759.

Neoverse V2 at 2^14 (perf, `lc-arm`): Euclid 16%, `Values<neon::Vec>` join 14% and split 10.7%,
`Values<Neon>` split (forward_upper's, a pass of its own after the transform) 8.1%, combine 5.6%,
apply's products 4.8%: split and join are a third of the time there too (#98, item 2).
