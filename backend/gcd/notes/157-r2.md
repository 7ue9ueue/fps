## Copies into and out of the transforms (backend/ntt: two overloads, `ntt/notes/157-r2.md`)

- `Transform::forward_upper(std::span in, H, Form::values)`: the upper half reads the entry in place
  (the variants' `forward_upper_from`), then the split; `extend` no longer copies the entry into
  the transform. For an entry of half + 1 words, its word 0 is e[0] - e[half] meanwhile.
- `Transform::inverse(F, work, out, form)`: the inverse through the caller's work array, the top
  level writing out (the variants' inverse from `from` through `f` to `out`, which `inverse(F,
  out)` runs in this thread's region): the combine's products go straight to the kept transform
  (one store stream, not two) and its inverse into the entry; apply's inverses write c and d
  (and the low products only their `low` words).
- `products2`: two sums of products in one pass sharing a pair of factors (apply's c and d; a row
  of the combine): 6 loads per word, not 8. Not timed alone.
- The results' scaling (monic gcd, h = R[0][1] / c) by Shoup products on vectors, not a 64-bit
  remainder per word; Euclid's six arrays in one zeroed block. Not timed alone.

Profile after (same machine, the final tree): inverse_mod at 2^14 2.38 ms (Euclid 22.6%, libc
0.8%), at 2^20 333 ms (libc 2.1%, combine 5.8%, apply's products 4.5%).

## For later

- Zen 5's steps stay near 55 cycles at k <= 32 whatever the work (13-14 ns at k = 16 and 32): a
  chain, not throughput. Windows of up to 2 zmm (k <= 16) could live in registers across steps
  (no store and load per step); at k = 64 they do not fit (8 zmm per remainder).
- `forward_upper`'s split runs as a pass of its own (5-6% on Emerald Rapids and Neoverse V2 at 2^14
  and 2^20); in the subtrees' tiles it would save its memory pass, not its arithmetic.
- The products could run in the inverse's tiles (a values-form `inverse_hooked`), saving a pass
  over each product at 2^20 (combine 5.8% and apply's products 4.5% there).
- `lc-intel` was noisy in this round (paired IQRs up to 1.07 where medians agreed within 1-2%).
